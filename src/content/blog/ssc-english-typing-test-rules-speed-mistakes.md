Conducted on the centralized **TCS iON Examination Engine**, the English typing test tests candidate speed, rhythm, and structural accuracy under rigorous timed conditions. Understanding the official Staff Selection Commission calculation formulas and software interface is critical to qualifying on your first attempt.

## 1. SSC CGL vs SSC CHSL: English Typing Differences

While both examinations use identical evaluation algorithms, their time duration and speed thresholds differ significantly:

### 1. SSC CHSL English Typing Test (10 Minutes)
* **Speed Target**: 35 WPM / 10,500 KDPH.
* **Total Keystrokes Required**: Exactly **1,750 key depressions** within 10 minutes.
* **Candidate Challenge**: Because 10 minutes is a short duration, initial nervousness or finger trembling directly impacts the first 2 minutes. You must complete the entire 1,750-keystroke master passage and utilize the remaining 60-90 seconds for error correction.

### 2. SSC CGL DEST Typing Test (15 Minutes)
* **Speed Target**: 27 WPM / 8,000 KDPH.
* **Total Keystrokes Required**: **2,000 key depressions** within 15 minutes.
* **Post-Specific Cutoffs**: While basic qualifying posts allow up to 20% errors for UR, prestigious departments like **Tax Assistant (CBIC & CBDT)** and **Executive Assistant** mandate a strict **5% error cutoff for UR** and **7% for OBC/EWS/SC/ST**.

## 2. Official SSC English Typing Mistake Evaluation Guidelines

SSC categorizes typing errors into two distinct mathematical categories: **Full Mistakes** and **Half Mistakes**.

### 🔴 Full Mistakes (1.0 Penalty Each)
A complete 1.0 word deduction is applied in the following scenarios:
1. **Omission of Word**: Skipping an entire word or number present in the master passage.
2. **Substitution of Word**: Typing an incorrect word in place of the original word (e.g., typing *"government"* instead of *"parliament"*).
3. **Addition of Extra Word**: Inserting a word or figure not present in the master text.

### 🟡 Half Mistakes (0.5 Penalty Each)
A 0.5 word deduction is applied for minor structural errors:
1. **Spacing Errors**: Omitting a required space between two words (e.g., *"thecourt"*), or inserting multiple unneeded spaces.
2. **Capitalization Errors**: Typing lowercase instead of uppercase or vice-versa (e.g., typing *"india"* instead of *"India"*).
3. **Punctuation Errors**: Omitting, adding, or misplacing commas (`,`), periods (`.`), semi-colons (`;`), or hyphens (`-`).
4. **Spelling / Transposition Errors**: Typing characters in reversed order or omitting single characters (e.g., typing *"recieved"* instead of *"received"*).

### 🧮 The Official SSC Mistake Percentage Formula

`Total Errors = Full Mistakes + (Half Mistakes × 0.5)`

`Percentage Error (%) = ( (Total Errors / Total Words in Master Passage) ) × 100`

If your calculated error percentage exceeds the official category cutoff, you are declared **disqualified** in the skill test, irrespective of high Tier-1 or Tier-2 written marks.

## 3. TCS iON Software Interface: Real Exam Hall Experience

The typing test at TCS iON centers operates with specific software constraints:

1. **Split-Screen Display**: The master English text appears in the upper half of the monitor screen, while your input textbox is in the lower half.
2. **No Word Highlighting**: Unlike casual typing web apps that highlight current words in green or red, **TCS iON provides no highlighting or auto-correction**. You must manually maintain visual tracking between the screen sections.
3. **Vertical Scroll Bar**: The master passage contains 300 to 400 words, requiring candidates to scroll the window downwards using either the mouse wheel or the scrollbar.
4. **Active Backspace**: Backspace and arrow keys remain active throughout the examination. You can scroll up to previously typed lines and fix spelling mistakes as long as time remains on the countdown timer.
5. **Auto-Submission**: When the 10-minute (CHSL) or 15-minute (CGL) timer reaches `00:00`, the software automatically freezes your text and submits the input.

## 4. The "Speed Buffer" Strategy: Why 35 WPM at Home is Not Enough

Most candidates who score 35-37 WPM on practice software fail on exam day due to the **"Exam Hall Shock Factor"**:
* **Acoustic Noise**: In a hall with 200+ candidates typing simultaneously, the sound of mechanical and membrane keyboard clatter creates significant auditory distraction.
* **Stiff Keyboards**: Examination center keyboards (often TVS-e, Dell KB216, or HP membrane boards) have varying key travel and tactile resistance compared to smooth laptop keyboards.
* **Hand Trembling**: Adrenaline and nervous tension cause hand and finger stiffness during the first 120 seconds.

### The Golden Rule:
> **Target 42–45 WPM in English practice tests with 98%+ accuracy** to comfortably cruise through the 35 WPM exam requirement with under 3% error rate.
