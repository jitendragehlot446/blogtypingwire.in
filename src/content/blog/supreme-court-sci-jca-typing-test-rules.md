The **Supreme Court of India (SCI)** conducts one of the strictest and most prestigious typing examinations in the country for recruitment to the post of **Junior Court Assistant (JCA) - Group 'B' Non-Gazetted**.

While many exams allow 5% to 10% mistakes, the Supreme Court of India imposes a **strict 3% error ceiling (97% minimum accuracy)**, making it a true test of surgical precision.

## 1. The 3% Error Formula: Why SCI JCA is So Competitive

In standard typing exams, a candidate typing 400 words can commit 20 to 28 mistakes (5% to 7%) and still qualify. In the Supreme Court JCA exam:

`Maximum Allowed Errors = (3 / 100) × Total Words Typed`

* If you type **350 words**, you are allowed at most **10 mistakes**. If you commit 11 mistakes, you are disqualified instantly.
* If you type **450 words**, you are allowed at most **13.5 mistakes**.

### Evaluation Criteria:
1. **Full Mistake (1 Error)**:
   - Every omitted word from the judgment draft.
   - Every substituted or misspelled word.
   - Every addition of a word not found in the original passage.
2. **Half Mistake (0.5 Error)**:
   - Punctuation errors (e.g., missing semicolons or quotation marks common in legal citations).
   - Capitalization errors (e.g., typing *court* instead of *Court*).
   - Irregular spacing between words.

## 2. Supreme Court Typing Interface & Software Mechanics

* **Exam Delivery Platform**: TCS iON / Prometric style secure lockdown browser.
* **Layout**: Upper half displays the legal passage; lower half is the active candidate typing area.
* **Auto-Scrolling**: The passage auto-scrolls line by line as the typist progresses.
* **Editing Capabilities**: Candidates can backspace within words and navigate across typed lines using arrow keys.
