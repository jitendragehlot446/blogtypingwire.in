## 1. The 3 In-the-Moment Emergency Resets

If you feel nervous tremors creeping into your fingers while seated at your exam terminal:

### 🫁 1. The 4-7-8 Breathing Reset (Activates Parasympathetic System)
1. Close your eyes for 15 seconds before the test starts.
2. Inhale quietly through your nose for a count of **4 seconds**.
3. Hold your breath for **7 seconds**.
4. Exhale audibly through your mouth for **8 seconds**.
5. Repeat 3 times. This forces your vagus nerve to slow your cardiac pacemaker, instantly stopping physiological finger tremors.

### 🤲 2. The Palm Friction & Tendon Warm-Up
Government exam computer labs are often aggressively air-conditioned. Cold hands reduce tactile nerve sensitivity by up to 30%:
* Vigorously rub your palms together for 20 seconds to generate surface friction heat.
* Make tight fists for 3 seconds, then spread your fingers wide. Repeat 5 times to pump warm blood directly into your fingertips.

### 🧘 3. The 5-4-3-2-1 Sensory Grounding Technique
If your mind starts racing with thoughts of failure:
* Mentally name **5 things you see** (the monitor bezel, your keyboard cord, the desk edge).
* Feel **4 physical textures** (the keycaps under your fingertips, your chair back).
* Hear **3 distinct sounds** (the hum of the AC, the invigilator walking).
This immediately halts mental panic by anchoring your prefrontal cortex in physical reality.

## 2. Strategic Rules for the Live 10-Minute Test

### 🐢 The "Slow Start" Rule (First 60 Seconds)
The biggest mistake candidates make is sprinting at 50 WPM the microsecond the clock starts. If you make 3 typos in the first 10 words, your brain enters panic mode.
* **The Rule**: Intentionally type the first 25–30 words at a relaxed, measured **28–30 WPM pace**.
* Once your hands confirm that the keys are registering correctly and your heart rate stabilizes, your natural 40+ WPM rhythm will take over effortlessly.

### 🎧 Overcoming the "Keyboard Chorus" Auditory Shock
The moment the test begins, 150 candidates will begin furiously slamming their keyboards simultaneously. The deafening noise creates an illusion that everyone else is typing twice as fast as you.
* **Mental Armor**: Remember that loud, aggressive typists usually have terrible accuracy and are burning through their allowed mistake quota. 
* Block out the room. Focus your vision exclusively on the **current word highlighted on your screen**.
