Most government examination notifications in India — including **SSC CGL DEST, CHSL, High Courts, and Police HCM** — specify speed requirements in **Key Depressions Per Hour (KDPH)** or **Words Per Minute (WPM)**.

Confusing keystroke counts with word counts causes many aspirants to misjudge their speed benchmarks during practice. This mathematical guide provides the **exact conversion derivations, shortcut formulas, mistake deduction formulas, and exam-wise conversion matrices**.

## 1. The 5-Keystroke Standard & Mathematical Derivation

Why does typing software not simply count dictionary words?
* Words like `a`, `of`, `the` have 1 to 3 letters, while words like `constitutional`, `administration` have 14+ letters. Counting raw dictionary words would unfairly penalize candidates who receive passages with complex legal or administrative vocabulary.
* To standardize scoring, international typing standards and the Government of India define **1 Standard Word = 5 Key Depressions (Keystrokes)**, including spacebar hits and punctuation marks.

### 📐 The Mathematical Proof:
$$\text{Words Per Hour} = \frac{\text{Key Depressions Per Hour (KDPH)}}{5}$$

Since there are 60 minutes in an hour:
$$\text{Speed in WPM} = \frac{\text{Words Per Hour}}{60} = \frac{\text{KDPH}}{5 \times 60} = \mathbf{\frac{\text{KDPH}}{300}}$$

> [!TIP]
> **The 300 Rule**: To instantly convert any KDPH figure to Words Per Minute, simply divide by **300**!
> * $8,000 \text{ KDPH} \div 300 = \mathbf{26.67 \text{ WPM}}$
> * $10,500 \text{ KDPH} \div 300 = \mathbf{35.00 \text{ WPM}}$
> * $15,000 \text{ KDPH} \div 300 = \mathbf{50.00 \text{ WPM}}$

## 2. Gross Speed vs Net Speed Calculation Formulas

Commissions evaluate your performance based on **Net Speed** after deducting mistake penalties:

### 🔹 1. Gross Speed (Raw WPM)
$$\text{Gross Words} = \frac{\text{Total Key Depressions Typed}}{5}$$

$$\text{Gross Speed (WPM)} = \frac{\text{Gross Words}}{\text{Test Duration in Minutes}}$$

### 🔹 2. Net Speed (Qualifying WPM)
Different exam agencies enforce different penalty factors:

#### SSC & Railway Evaluation Model:
$$\text{Total Errors} = \text{Full Mistakes} + \left( \frac{\text{Half Mistakes}}{2} \right)$$
$$\text{Percentage Error (\%)} = \left( \frac{\text{Total Errors} \times 100}{\text{Total Words in Master Passage}} \right)$$

#### Police & Paramilitary Net Speed Model (10-Word Penalty per Mistake):
$$\text{Net Speed (WPM)} = \frac{\text{Gross Words} - (\text{Mistakes} \times 10)}{\text{Duration in Minutes}}$$

*Example*: If you type 400 gross words in a 10-minute Delhi Police exam (40 Gross WPM) but make 5 mistakes:
$$\text{Net Speed} = \frac{400 - (5 \times 10)}{10} = \frac{400 - 50}{10} = \mathbf{35 \text{ Net WPM}}$$
Those 5 mistakes cost you **5 full WPM** and critical merit points!

## 3. Key Depressions Checklist: What Counts as a Keystroke?

* ✅ **Alphabet Characters (`A-Z`, `a-z`)**: 1 Key Depression each.
* ✅ **Numeric Digits (`0-9`)**: 1 Key Depression each.
* ✅ **Punctuation (`. , : ; " ' - ! ?`)**: 1 Key Depression each.
* ✅ **Spacebar**: 1 Key Depression each.
* ❌ **Backspace Key**: Does **NOT** count toward total key depressions (it only burns time).
* ❌ **Shift Key**: Does **NOT** count as an extra depression (e.g. typing capital `T` counts as 1 key depression, not 2).
* ❌ **Enter Key**: Handled as 1 depression or ignored depending on whether the test features auto-wrap.
