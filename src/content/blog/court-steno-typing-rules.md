The **Stenographer Skill Test** across the **Staff Selection Commission (SSC Steno Grade C & D)**, **Supreme Court**, **High Courts (PA / Senior Steno)**, and **State Legislative Assemblies** is fundamentally different from a standard data entry typing test.

Candidates must first record a fast audio dictation in **shorthand (Pitman or Gregg system)** and then accurately transcribe their handwritten shorthand notes onto a computer terminal within a specified time limit.

## 1. The Two-Stage Skill Test Process

The skill test consists of two contiguous phases:

1. **Phase 1: Audio Dictation (Shorthand Recording)**:
   - An examiner reads aloud a speech, parliamentary debate, or legal judgment at a constant cadence (80 or 100 WPM).
   - Before the actual dictation, a **1 to 2-minute trial dictation** is conducted to allow candidates to check room acoustics, audio levels, and speaker clarity.
   - Candidates record the passage into their physical shorthand notebook using pencil or fountain pen.
   - After dictation ends, candidates are given **10 minutes of reading time** to review their shorthand outlines, punctuate sentences, and resolve ambiguous glyphs before touching the computer.

2. **Phase 2: Computer Transcription**:
   - Candidates sit at computer terminals to transcribe their shorthand notes into English or Hindi text.
   - The computer screen provides a blank text editor. Candidates read from their physical notepad and type directly into the software.

## 2. Official Mistake Classification Rules

Recruitment commissions evaluate transcriptions using strict typographical penalty criteria:

### 🔴 Full Mistakes (100% Penalty / 1.0 Deduction)
1. **Omission of Words**: Leaving out any word, phrase, or sentence that was dictated.
2. **Substitution of Words**: Replacing the dictated word with an incorrect word (e.g. typing *parliament* instead of *president*).
3. **Addition of Words**: Inserting words that were not spoken in the official audio dictation.
4. **Spelling Mistakes**: Misspelling any word according to standard dictionary rules.
5. **Incomplete Words**: Leaving a word partially typed at the expiration of the timer.

### 🟡 Half Mistakes (50% Penalty / 0.5 Deduction)
1. **Capitalization Errors**: Using lowercase letters for proper nouns (e.g., `india` instead of `India`) or beginning sentences with a lowercase letter.
2. **Punctuation Discrepancies**: Missing full stops, commas, question marks, or inverted commas where explicitly dictated or grammatically required.
3. **Transposition of Words**: Swapping the order of adjacent words (e.g., typing *only court* instead of *court only*).
4. **Singular vs Plural Mismatches**: Typing a singular form instead of plural (e.g., `officer` instead of `officers`).
5. **Compound Words**: Spacing words incorrectly (e.g., typing `some times` instead of `sometimes`).

## 3. Stenographer Error Calculation Equation

$$\text{Total Mistakes} = \text{Full Mistakes} + \left( \frac{\text{Half Mistakes}}{2} \right)$$

$$\text{Error Percentage (\%)} = \left( \frac{\text{Total Mistakes} \times 100}{\text{Total Words Dictated}} \right)$$

### 📊 Real Example (SSC Steno Grade D):
* Total words dictated: **800 Words**
* Permissible error limit for General (UR): **7%** $\rightarrow$ Maximum allowable mistakes = $\frac{800 \times 7}{100} = \mathbf{56 \text{ Mistakes}}$
* Candidate's record: 42 Full Mistakes, 20 Half Mistakes
* Total calculated mistakes: $42 + (20 / 2) = 42 + 10 = \mathbf{52 \text{ Mistakes}}$
* Candidate's Error %: $\frac{52 \times 100}{800} = \mathbf{6.5\%}$
* **Result**: **QUALIFIED** ✅ (Under 7% ceiling).

## 4. Key Rules for Court Stenography (High Court / District Courts)

High Court examinations feature additional hurdles that central SSC candidates do not face:
1. **Legal Terminology**: Passages are dense with legal jargon (*petitioner, respondent, appellant, impugned, certiorari, writ petition, bail application*). Candidates must prepare specialized shorthand outlines for common Indian legal terminology.
2. **Stricter Error Cutoffs**: While SSC allows up to 7% or 10% errors, many High Courts (e.g., Patna, Allahabad, Delhi) enforce a strict **3% to 5% maximum error limit**.
3. **Speed Minimum on Computer**: In addition to shorthand transcription, several state courts require candidates to clear an independent **40 WPM general typing test**.

## 6. Daily Shorthand & Transcription Practice Routine

1. **Daily Audio Transcription**: Transcribe at least one full 10-minute audio passage daily under strict timer constraints.
2. **Read Back Out Loud**: Always read back your shorthand notes out loud immediately after recording to verify outline readability.
3. **Audit Your Typos**: Count full and half mistakes using the official formula. If your error rate exceeds 5%, slow down your shorthand handwriting to write cleaner, more legible outlines.
